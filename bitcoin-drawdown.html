What this page will not do

It will not tell you whether the low above is the bottom. It will not name a month, a price or a target, here or anywhere else on this site. Three finished cycles are three data points, they differ by 42 days and by eight percentage points of depth, and the market that traded them is not this one.

A drawdown figure is arithmetic about the past. Search this question and most results will hand you a prediction wrapped around the number. That is the part we leave out.

Why other sites show a different number

You will find the all-time high quoted as 126,198 on 6 October 2025. We put the top on the same day and carry a different price. That number is an intraday print from a single exchange, and measuring against it makes every drawdown on this page look about one percentage point shallower. Ours is a full-day price: one number per UTC day, the same number for everyone who looks at the same day.

The same choice moves some dates. An intraday source puts the 2013 top on 30 November 2013; the highest full-day price came four days later, on 4 December. We use the daily series, and we say so rather than leaving you to work out which one you are reading.

How we count

Every historical price on this page comes from PulseHawk's own archive of 5,849 daily Bitcoin prices from 17 August 2010 onward, built from the blockchain.com market price series, which is one cross-exchange price per UTC day rather than one venue's closing trade. The drawdown at the top of the page is measured against the most recent price in our daily market log, which is written every day at 21:23 UTC and is stamped on the page with its own date. A cycle top is the highest daily price before the next cycle's top. The maximum drawdown is the lowest daily price after that top divided by the top, and the day count is calendar days between the two, with the top as day zero.

Nothing on this page is copied from CoinGecko's tables, CoinMarketCap, Bitcoin Magazine or any other site's drawdown page. The two dates under the two big numbers are there so you can check both against any daily chart.
