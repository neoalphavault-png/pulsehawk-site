Zcash NU7 on testnet, block times measured

Yes, the Zcash testnet after NU7 runs close to 25 second blocks. Over the 49,203 testnet blocks since activation the mean interval was 23.68 s against the 25 s target, and the spread stayed close to an ideal 25 s chain, apart from one gap of 451 s. Measured independently up to 18 October 2026, 06:00 UTC, with 500 of 500 sampled hashes matching two Zebra peers.

Does NU7 hit 25 second blocks on testnet?

Yes. The mean interval was 23.68 s, so blocks came about 5% faster than the target. The median of 17.0 s is what a 25 s chain should show, because most blocks arrive early and a few arrive late.

How often is a block slower than the old 75 s target?

In 3.59% of intervals, against 4.98% for an ideal 25 s chain. Before NU7 the target itself was 75 s. Gaps over 150 s came in 0.203% of intervals, against 0.248% expected.

The longest gap was 451 s, longer than the roughly 284 s an ideal chain would show over the same number of blocks. No block timestamp went backwards.

Our own measurement, Zcash NU7 testnet, headers from testnet peers, blocks 4,465,026 to 4,514,228, snapshot 18 October 2026, 06:00 UTC.

How fast does difficulty follow hashrate?

We found 6 hashrate jumps of 2x or more, and 5 of them caught up. The other one was followed by the next jump 200 blocks later, before it could catch up. For those five the difficulty came within ±15 % of the new level after a median of 960 blocks, or about 2.7 hours when measured in time, and the longest catch-up took 1,839 blocks.

Our own measurement, Zcash NU7 testnet, headers from testnet peers, blocks 4,465,026 to 4,514,228, snapshot 18 October 2026, 06:00 UTC.

How long do confirmations take?

Thirty blocks at 25 s spanned a median of 730 s and a p99 of 1,187 s. For perfect 25 s blocks the p99 would be about 1,105 s, so the measured value sits above that, partly because of the hashrate jumps (1,154 s without the windows around them), and still well below the 1,409 s for ten blocks at 75 s.

Our own measurement, Zcash NU7 testnet, headers from testnet peers, blocks 4,465,026 to 4,514,228, snapshot 18 October 2026, 06:00 UTC.

How many blocks were orphaned?

From 17 October 2026, 14:51 to 20:21 UTC our listener recorded every block announced to it. Five of the 780 did not land in the chain, 0.64%, and each of them at depth 1.

Our own listener, Zcash NU7 testnet, window 17 October 2026, 14:51 to 20:21 UTC.

How we measured

Our own header client followed the Zcash testnet from the NU7 activation at block 4,465,026 to block 4,514,228, the snapshot of 18 October 2026, 06:00 UTC, fetching headers directly from testnet peers and checking hash linkage and proof of work. That is 49,203 blocks. We checked 500 of them against two Zebra peers, and all 500 hashes matched, so we saw no fork in the blocks we compared.

Intervals come from consecutive block timestamps. Method in How we measure, version 1.

Download the raw intervals

The file comes from our own header client, which fetched every block header directly from testnet peers over P2P, checked the hash linkage and the proof of work against nBits, and compared 500 hashes with two further Zebra peers. It does not check block contents or Equihash solutions, and it does not come from nodes of our own.

  • height is the block height on the Zcash testnet, from 4,465,026 to 4,514,228.
  • timestamp is the block header timestamp in Unix seconds, which count in UTC.
  • interval_s is that timestamp minus the timestamp of the parent block, in seconds. It stays empty for 4,465,026, because the first counted interval is that of 4,465,027.

Questions we get asked

Is the Zcash NU7 testnet producing 25 second blocks?

Yes, close to it. Up to 18 October 2026, 06:00 UTC the mean block interval was 23.68 s over 49,203 blocks, and median, p90 and p99 sat a few seconds below an ideal 25 s chain.

Do shorter blocks mean more orphaned blocks?

In our listener window on 17 October 2026, 14:51 to 20:21 UTC, 5 of 780 announced blocks did not land in the chain, 0.64%, each at depth 1.

How many confirmations match the old 10 at 75 s?

Thirty blocks at 25 s take the same 750 s on average as ten blocks at 75 s. Measured, they spanned a median of 730 s and a p99 of 1,187 s, well below the 1,409 s for ten blocks at 75 s.
