What PulseHawk is

PulseHawk is a market data feed built on one uncomfortable observation: most of what circulates as market information cannot be checked by the person reading it. Price targets nobody revisits, "whale alerts" without a wallet address, narratives that dissolve the moment you ask for the underlying number.

We publish the opposite. A feed where every number comes with a receipt, a source you can open, an address you can look up, a dataset you can download. On-chain flows, large wallet movements, supply data and the market context around them, published the moment they happen and archived so anyone can verify what we said and when we said it.

The rules we publish by

receipts or it does not exist

A number without a source does not get published. Every alert links to the raw data behind it. If we counted something ourselves, we show how.

movement, never motive

The chain shows that coins moved. It does not show why, and it does not show who. A deposit to an exchange is not proof of a sale, and we will never pretend otherwise. We report what happened, you decide what it means.

no hype, no price targets

We do not predict prices, we do not shill assets and we do not do partnerships that would give us a reason to. A data feed is only worth following if it has nothing to sell you.

the feed stays free

Alerts and core data will always be free to follow. That is not generosity, it is the model. A feed people rely on is the product.

Where we start

The first format is live now. Every Sunday we publish where the market stands inside the Bitcoin cycle, counted in days from the halving to the cycle top, next to the three cycles before it. Two more formats follow over the coming weeks, and the cadence grows from there. Everything we publish is dated and archived, so it can be checked later.

Coverage begins where verification is strongest and attention is thinnest: on-chain data for proof-of-work and emerging networks that the big analytics platforms ignore. Whale wallet tracking, exchange flow analysis, supply and emission data, all monitored around the clock by automated systems and published as alerts, daily numbers and weekly readings. From there, coverage expands one market at a time, only when we can meet our own standard for it.

Data pages

Every weekly format also gets a permanent page. The graphic goes out on the feed, the page keeps the numbers current and shows where each one came from.
