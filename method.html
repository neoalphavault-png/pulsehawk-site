How we measure

Every number we publish comes from our own measurement, inside a stated window, with a method anyone can repeat. This page lists what we count, the rules we publish by and what falls outside our work. Method version 1, 16 October 2026.

How to cite

PulseHawk (@pulsehawkio), measurement method version 1, published 16 October 2026, pulsehawk.io/method#version

Using our numbers? Tag @pulsehawkio and we add you to the list.

Where do the numbers come from?

We measure ourselves, from full nodes we run or from our own header clients connected directly to network peers. Each report says which. We do not publish their addresses, hostnames or locations, so nobody can tune a network around our measurement.

Third party explorers serve as a cross check, never as the source of a published number.

What is a measurement window?

Each measurement has a fixed start and end in UTC, agreed before it starts. Before and after comparisons use two windows of the same length. Every published number names its window and the method version it was counted with.

What do we count?

  • Blocks per second and per minute.
  • Block time distribution. Median, p90, p99 and the longest gap between two blocks.
  • Transactions per minute.
  • Confirmation and acceptance time. From first sight of a transaction to its first block, and to the depth the network counts as final.
  • Fees. Per transaction and per block.
  • Stale and orphaned blocks. The stale block rate in % of all blocks seen.
  • Propagation delay. The gap between the first and the last of our measurement points to see a block.
  • Difficulty adjustment. How fast difficulty follows a change in hashrate.

What are the rules we publish by?

No source, no number

A number without its window and raw data does not get published. If we counted something ourselves, we show how.

What happened, not why

A chain shows what the network did. It does not show intent. We report blocks and transactions, readers draw their own conclusions.

No price statements

No price targets, no buy or sell calls, and no deals that would give us a reason for either.

Public results stay free

Published measurements and their raw data stay free to read and download.

What is in a report?

  1. Summary. The answer in a few sentences, with dates.
  2. Window. Start and end in UTC, network, node software versions.
  3. Charts and tables. Median, p90, p99 and the longest gap, before and after.
  4. Method. The method version used, with any deviation named.
  5. Raw data. A CSV with every block and timestamp we counted.

The first public sample report is Zcash NU7 on testnet, published on 18 October 2026.

What do we not do?

We do not review or audit code. We do not audit security. We make no statements about prices. We do not run validators or mine for clients, and we never publish node IPs or hostnames.

Questions we get asked

What is a stale block rate?

The share of valid blocks that did not end up in the main chain, in % of all blocks we saw in the window. If it rises after an upgrade, blocks reach parts of the network too late.

How do you measure propagation delay?

Each of our measurement points records when a block first arrives. The gap between the first and the last point is the propagation delay for that block. We report median and p90 for the window.

Can we repeat your measurement?

Yes. The method is on this page and the raw data is in the CSV. Two people with the same window should get the same numbers.
